Safety and privacy

What Kaaka does to keep callers' data safe, and what it cannot do.

By design

  • Works without data. Calls and SMS only. A rejected call costs the caller nothing.
  • Only the Kaaka number can start the beacon. Kaaka Sensors reacts to an SMS from the configured number and nothing else; a stranger cannot make a phone report its location.
  • Reports go only to the Kaaka number. Never to another number.
  • The control room is locked before it goes public. The server refuses a public URL without an admin token. Only the location link page and its endpoint are public, and each link expires after 24 hours and accepts one update per 10 seconds.
  • Rate limiting per caller stops one number from flooding responders.
  • Nothing sensitive in backups. Both apps opt out of Android cloud backup, since they hold phone numbers, tokens and SMS.
  • Every reply names 112. Kaaka is an addition to the emergency number, never a replacement.

What is stored, and where

WhereWhat
Kaaka number (app)Settings, the last 200 events with caller numbers, and the ordinary SMS inbox.
Control room data directoryEvery event: numbers, message texts, alert reports, coordinates, who acknowledged and resolved. The responder registry with names, numbers and home positions.
Caller's phone (Kaaka Sensors)Its own settings and the SMS it sent.

Treat the data directory as sensitive: restrict who can read it, back it up somewhere equally restricted, and delete history you no longer need.

What Kaaka cannot do

  • It cannot talk to the caller. Android gives apps no call audio; Kaaka only screens, rejects and texts.
  • It cannot locate a caller who does not share their location, either with Kaaka Sensors or the link.
  • Fall detection is a simple, untuned heuristic.
  • Distance and ETA are straight-line estimates at an assumed city speed.
  • SMS can be delayed or lost by the network. Responders should still call the caller back.
